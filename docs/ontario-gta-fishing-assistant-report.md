## What your Sport licence means

Ontario states that a Sport Fishing Licence is for anglers who want full catch and possession privileges. In the Fishing Regulations Summary, `S` means the Sport licence limit; for example, `S-4` means a catch and possession limit of 4.

You still need to follow the specific FMZ/waterbody/species rules. A Sport licence does not override closed seasons, size restrictions, sanctuaries, bait rules, or park rules.

Carry your Outdoors Card and Licence Summary if the licence is not printed on the card or the card has not arrived. Ontario says a digital licence summary must be the official PDF, not a screenshot.

## Toronto/GTA FMZ guide

- **FMZ 20**: generally Lake Ontario water — Toronto waterfront, harbours/bays, Ashbridges Bay, Toronto Islands, Tommy Thompson Park, Humber Bay, Colonel Samuel Smith Park.
- **FMZ 16**: generally rivers/creeks flowing into Lake Ontario — Humber River, Don River, Rouge River, Credit River, Etobicoke Creek, Highland Creek.
- Boundary matters: the same outing can put you near both FMZ 20 lake/bay water and FMZ 16 tributary water.

## Legal and safety cautions

- City of Toronto says shore anglers can only use one rod and reel; no nets or other devices.
- Posted signs determine where fishing is or is not allowed on Toronto public lands.
- FMZ 16 is part of the Southern Bait Management Zone. Live/dead baitfish and leech movement has restrictions.
- FMZ 20 is Great Lakes water with its own bait movement rules.
- TRCA conservation areas have a no-live-bait policy; worms and artificial lures are the practical default unless a specific TRCA property says otherwise.
- Community fishing apps/forums can help with current bite reports, but they are not legal sources for seasons, limits, or licence rules.
- Legal to keep does not mean advisable to eat. Check Ontario’s Guide to Eating Fish by location, species, and size.
- Lake sturgeon is closed all year in FMZ 16 and FMZ 20. American eel is protected in FMZ 20. Warmouth is endangered in FMZ 16.
